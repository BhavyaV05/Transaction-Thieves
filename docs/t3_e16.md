# E16: planner distillation (QLoRA student vs the same model prompted vs the teacher)

Owner: T3 (Akshat). Spec: `plan.md` section 6.11 and the experiment table (E16: "QLoRA student vs same model prompted vs teacher vs E18 backbone", secondary), `tracks.md` section 5.4 step 4. Numbers by `scripts/t3_e16_report.py` (CPU, validation only, cluster bootstrap seed 0, 2,000 resamples; JSON in `outputs/t3_val/analysis/e16.json`, gitignored). The test split was not touched.

**Question.** Does a QLoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-8B, trained on filtered planner steps of the Gemma 4 12B teacher, plan better than the same Qwen3-8B prompted, and does it match the 12B teacher?

## 3. Interpretation

- **Does the student beat the prompted 8B?** Marginally in the point estimate, not in a way the data can support. All three seeds are 0.003 to 0.006 Brier better than the prompted model (mean 0.2244 vs 0.2289), but the paired 95% CI of each difference spans zero by about +-0.018. The seed-to-seed sd (0.0016) is small, but the seeds share the same 252 examples, so it understates the uncertainty about a new training set. Reading: no detectable gain from distillation on this budget.
- **Does it match the Gemma 12B teacher?** No. Gemma ours_compose is at 0.2014 (+0.0029 vs p0, CI includes zero); the students are at +0.024 to +0.027 with intervals that exclude zero, roughly 0.023 Brier worse. The gap between the 8B arms and Gemma is ten times the gap between student and prompted.
- **Does anything beat p0?** No. p0 alone is 0.1985; Gemma ties it (not significantly worse), and the prompted model and all three students are significantly worse (CI above zero for every Qwen row). AUC tells the same story: p0 0.760, Gemma 0.753, students 0.72 to 0.74, none significantly different from p0 in ranking, but all in the same direction (student minus p0 AUC about -0.03).
- **Why.** This agrees with `docs/t3_val_analysis.md`: the answers carry no signal beyond p0 under the back-test LRs, and every update moves p away from a good prior. Gemma stays close to p0 because it mostly asks questions the back-test did not accept (accepted share 0.18, so only 45 of 261 questions update the fusion; 33% of alerts move, mean shift 0.026). Qwen, prompted or tuned, picks accepted questions almost every time (1.00 prompted, 0.77 to 0.91 student), each of which updates the fusion with a non-trivial LR, so 95 to 97% of alerts move, with mean shift 0.09 to 0.11. Among the alerts that move, the move goes toward the label in only 38 to 42% of cases for every Qwen arm (Gemma 40%), so these moves are noise to slightly harmful, and a bigger shift hurts more. The student's small gain over the prompted model is consistent with this: it asks more questions (1.46 vs 1.04) but its accepted share and its shift are lower (0.86 vs 1.00; 0.089 vs 0.107), i.e. it stays slightly nearer to p0, which is the only direction that pays. The data do not show that the student learned a better question policy; a question filter that keeps only steps that moved toward the true label cannot teach a planner to predict that on new alerts when the answers are uninformative.
- **Caveats.** 252 training examples, no stop examples, 2 epochs; a different filter (e.g. keeping the whole teacher steps and not only the label-favourable ones) or more data was not tried. The Gemma row is the teacher's *system* (compose mode, same harness) on the val alerts, not the exact teacher runs on train. Train/serve quantisation mismatch (NF4 vs AWQ) may cost the student a little. n = 160 alerts with positives rare enough that all CIs are wide.

Reproduce: `.venv/bin/python scripts/t3_e16_report.py`. `scripts/val_runs_table.py` skips the `e16_*` directories on purpose: this is a different backbone, not a G4 candidate, so its rows stay out of `docs/t3_val_runs.md`.

This part covers the components that sit between the planner and the final probability: the gate that every question and action passes through, the privacy accountant it consults, the retrieval of rules and red flags, the formula that turns answers into a probability, the decision rule, and the reimplemented external agents of Groups C and D that T4 owns. Each sub-section ends with how the component is tested.

## 5.4.1 The compliance gate

The gate (`ftm/compliance/gate.py`, settings in `configs/compliance/gate.yaml`) is an allowlist in front of every tool call, and the same file is used for every system (our planner and every external agent), so that no system is helped or hindered by its rules (section 6.2). The default is deny: an action is allowed only if it is on the list (`ask_bank`, `draft_sar`, `escalate_to_human`, `lookup_rules`, `local_graph`, `search_library`, `stop`) and every check below passes. Requests for raw records (`request_records`, `share_records`) are blocked in every arm except the single baseline arm that measures what sharing raw records would buy (H2), where the setting `allow_raw_share` is switched on.

For `ask_bank` the checks run in a fixed order, and the first failure decides:

1. the partner is a known partner bank and not the home bank;
2. the privacy level is one of the allowed levels, ε ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2} (`eps_levels` in `gate.yaml`);
3. the program parses in the question language of section 5.2 (T2's validator is plugged in) and its answer kind (yes/no, count, k-ary choice, set count) is permitted by a legal basis that applies to the partner's jurisdiction;
4. the program contains no identifying literal: no account alias, no raw account number, no date. A question may refer to the alert only through its slots (`ALERT.receiver` and the like), so it cannot be turned into "tell me about account X on day Y";
5. a cited rule exists, applies to that jurisdiction and permits `ask_bank` for this answer kind. Citation is required for our planner and optional for the external agents (`require_citation` in `gate.yaml`), which keeps Group C and D agents from failing on a requirement their papers do not have;
6. the privacy accountant can pay for the question (section 5.4.2); otherwise the decision is `over_budget`, not `blocked`.

Decisions use the vocabulary of the question log (`allowed`, `blocked`, `over_budget`), so the harness and the evaluation read refusals from one place. `SAR` drafts and escalations are allowed only inside the home bank (rule `INTERNAL-SAR-DRAFT`); a draft addressed to another bank is blocked.

The legal bases are four rules in `configs/compliance/rules.yaml`: `US-314B-SHARE`, from 31 CFR 1010.540 [16]; `US-314B-NOTICE`, from FinCEN's fact sheet [17], which describes the notice and confidentiality duties and permits nothing by itself (so citing it is a wrong citation); `SG-COSMIC-REQUEST`, from the MAS COSMIC material [18]; and `INTERNAL-SAR-DRAFT`. The texts are short summaries for the planner prompt, not legal advice. Every AMLworld bank is synthetic, so by default each is treated as under 314(b); `jurisdiction_by_bank` can assign banks to Singapore, which is how the COSMIC rule is exercised in a sensitivity run.

**Nothing can skip the gate.** The harness toolbox (`ftm/harness/toolbox.py`, T2) is the only road from any system to a partner bank. It runs the gate, then the accountant, then the clock, then the bank, and it replaces the investigator's own account of its questions with the toolbox's log, so a system cannot misreport what it asked. Where the toolbox is not used (stand-alone tests), `GatedToolBox` wraps any toolbox with the same gate and charges the accountant once.

**How it is tested.** `tests/test_compliance_gate.py` holds a hand-written suite of 20 disallowed requests (an unknown action, sending funds, raw records, asking the home bank, an unknown partner, ε that is zero, negative, off the grid or too large, no program, an unparseable program, an alias, an account number or a date inside the program, an unknown rule, a rule from the wrong jurisdiction, a rule that permits nothing, a SAR or an escalation sent to another bank) and asserts that all of them are blocked. The share blocked is reported as {{TBD:gate_rag}}; the target is 100%.

## 5.4.2 Privacy accounting at the gate

The mechanisms that add noise to an answer, and the odds-shift ceiling each one implies, belong to the banks (section 5.2). What the gate needs from the accountant (`ftm/privacy/accountant.py`, T2) is a yes or no and a charge. The accountant keeps three limits, all by summed ε (basic composition):

- **per alert:** ε = 2 across every question asked about one alert, whichever bank is asked (the value H1 is tested at, `prereg/hypotheses.yaml`);
- **per partner account:** a cap across all alerts and all asking banks for the whole test period. A question is charged to every partner account whose transactions it can see: the subject for an account question, every member of the set for a set question. The cap is provisionally 8 and is revisited with the repeat-account statistics; on the real alert sets a cap of 8 refuses 1 to 3% of the questions of a stream of two ε = 1 questions per alert, a cap of 4 refuses 4 to 10% and a cap of 2 refuses 15 to 25% (`docs/t1_handoffs.md`, A3);
- **calibration:** back-testing spends from a separate ledger, ε_cal, which is reported on its own and never mixed into the per-alert budget.

The accountant is read, not written, by `Gate.check` (`can_spend`), and charged in exactly one place afterwards, and only when a reply comes back: a decline or a timeout releases nothing, so it costs nothing (`ftm/harness/toolbox.py`). Questions that return a customer record rather than a transaction-level quantity (`entity_accounts`) are listed as restricted and are outside the transaction-level guarantee (`prereg/hypotheses.yaml`). The guarantee protects one transaction, not one customer; section 10 returns to this.

**How it is tested.** The empirical likelihood ratio of each mechanism must not exceed its ceiling beyond sampling error; on the real HI-Medium back-test table the check covers 1,037 cells with no violation (`docs/t1_handoffs.md`, A4). The accountant refuses over-budget questions and enforces the per-account cap across alerts (T2's tests; summary in section 5.2).

## 5.4.3 Regulatory retrieval

The planner needs the legal basis for a question and, as inspiration, what the regulators describe as suspicious. The corpus (`configs/compliance/`) has the four rules above and 44 red flags: 28 from Appendix F of the FFIEC BSA/AML manual, copied verbatim from an Internet Archive snapshot [76], and 16 from Appendix B of the Guidelines to MAS Notice 626, paraphrased with the item number given [77]. FATF red-flag reports [19] are not in this version: the PDFs could not be fetched (`configs/compliance/red_flags.yaml`). Each red flag carries the AMLworld pattern types it relates to and a flag saying whether it can be measured from AMLworld fields (22 yes, 17 partly, 5 no), because the dataset has no KYC, income or geography; this is what ties the corpus to the expert question list (29 families, 56 questions, `configs/questions/expert_specs.yaml`), each of which names the red flags it comes from.

Retrieval (`ftm/compliance/rag.py`) is BM25 (k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75, a light stemmer, stop words removed) in plain Python, which is enough for a corpus of 48 short documents. The retriever can mix in a dense embedder as a hybrid, but none is configured in the default, so every retrieval number in this report is BM25 only. `lookup_rules` is a tool for every agent and returns hits filtered by jurisdiction. **Only the four rules are valid citations**: red flags are returned as context and the gate rejects them as a citation, so an agent cannot "cite" a red flag to justify a question. A citation is checked mechanically: the rule must exist, apply to the partner's jurisdiction and permit this answer kind. This checks that a cited rule is real and applicable; it does not check that a legal expert would agree with the rule's wording.

**How it is tested** (`ftm/compliance/rag_eval.py`, `scripts/rag_eval.py`, CPU only, seconds). Retrieval: for each askable expert question, the relevant documents are the red flags its spec names; we report recall and hit rate at k = 5 for BM25 against the v0 keyword match. Citation: 100 seeded cases (an askable expert question and a partner jurisdiction, US or Singapore) with the permitting rule as the expected answer; a system's cited rule is scored valid (passes the gate's own check) and exact (equals the expected id), and a missing citation is counted separately. The ablation from the plan puts the whole corpus into the prompt instead of the retrieved rules. The results are {{TBD:gate_rag}}.

## 5.4.4 Evidence fusion

Fusion (`ftm/fusion/fuse.py`) turns the home bank's score p0 and the back-tested likelihood ratios of the answers into one probability:

logit p = a · logit(p0) + Σ_f w_f · x_f + c,

where x_f is the sum of the log-likelihood ratios of the answers to questions of family f for this alert, taken from the back-test table and already clipped at the privacy ceiling. Only numbers go in: the function raises an error for non-numeric input, so no LLM text, and no reply note, can reach it (invariant 2 of section 3). A decline or a timeout adds no row and contributes nothing.

There are three modes. **Naive** (a = 1, w = 1, c = 0) is Bayes' rule with independent answers; it is the "summed log-LRs" ablation of Table 5b. **Shared** fits one weight for all families, with a and c. **Per-family** fits one weight per family. The fitted modes minimise a penalised log-loss on validation alerts (L-BFGS); the ridge pulls a toward 1 and the weights toward a prior, and the intercept c is free. Two priors exist: `naive` pulls w toward 1 ("trust the back-tested LR unless validation says otherwise") and `zero` pulls w toward 0 ("ignore the evidence unless validation supports it"). A family that was not seen in fitting gets the prior weight.

`fit_cv` chooses the ridge (1, 10, 100 or 1,000 pseudo-observations) and the prior (`naive` or `zero`) by 5-fold cross-validated Brier score, with every cluster of related alerts kept whole inside one fold, and ties going to the earlier, more cautious entry. It sees only the rows it is given and is called on validation alerts only, never on test. When it is scored on validation, an outer cross-fit wraps it, so a gain is never measured on rows that chose the setting. The chosen setting (fit mode, grid, priors, folds, seed, selection rule) is written into `prereg/` before gate G3 and is {{TBD:fusion_setting}}. The planner's stop rule uses the same function for the running posterior (`posterior`).

### What fusion did on real validation data

The first real-data check of the plan (gate G2, "the expert questions must improve Brier by at least 0.01 over p0 on validation") did not pass. The numbers below are gains over p0 in Brier score on the askable alerts of the validation `eval` part (alerts whose partner bank is one of the 30 modelled views; positive means fusion helps), with a 95% cluster-bootstrap interval. The sizes are small: 87 alerts (17 positive) for HI-Small, 160 (52 positive) for HI-Medium, with 23 accepted questions on HI-Medium and one on HI-Small.

| Dataset | Fusion | Gain over p0 [95% CI] | Target met |
|---|---|---|---|
| HI-Small | naive | -0.0053 [-0.0149, +0.0042] | no |
| HI-Small | per-family | -0.0063 [-0.0214, +0.0095] | no |
| HI-Small | per-family, CV-chosen (v1) | +0.0007 [-0.015, +0.019] | no |
| HI-Medium | naive | -0.0008 [-0.0135, +0.0125] | no |
| HI-Medium | per-family | +0.0129 [-0.0342, +0.0637] | point estimate only; CI covers 0 |
| HI-Medium | per-family, CV-chosen (v1) | +0.0053 [-0.041, +0.054] | no |

(The first, second, fourth and fifth rows are in `docs/t1_handoffs.md` A4. The CV-chosen rows come from the run of `scripts/g2_check.py --fusion per_family --fusion-select cv` with `scripts/fusion_diagnostics.py`. The CV-chosen rows and the diagnostic values are in `docs/t4_fusion_findings.md` section 6 (run by T3 on the reference machine, 26 Oct).) In the ε sweep on HI-Medium, asking more or adding no noise makes naive fusion worse (-0.0058 at ε = 2 and -0.0287 without noise; `docs/t4_fusion_findings.md`, section 1). The ceiling check passes everywhere, so the loss is not caused by the noise, and the planner that asks about one question per alert barely moves p0.

Three explanations were written down, each with its own table, before the diagnostics were run (`docs/t4_fusion_findings.md`): (1) *double counting*, because the one-bank model behind p0 already uses the patterns the questions measure; (2) *shift or winner's curse*, because the likelihood ratios are measured on train alerts, the same labels select which questions are accepted, and validation is later in time; (3) *calibration* of the fused posterior. The diagnostics, read together with their intervals, point to the second and not the first. The clearest example is the accepted question `dormant(ALERT.receiver, days=3)`: its back-tested likelihood ratio for the answer that fusion uses is 0.49 (evidence against laundering), while the same ratio measured on the validation answers is 1.15 (slightly for laundering), so the claimed and the validation direction disagree. The share of the evidence in the answers that p0 already holds, and the share of answers whose claimed and validation direction disagree, are {{TBD:fusion_diagnostics}}. These are validation numbers on few answers per cell, so the intervals are wide and we read the pattern as consistent with winner's curse, not as proved.

We therefore report naive fusion as a **pre-registered negative result**: its failure to reach G2 was a stated possibility with a stated test, and the failure is recorded rather than tuned away. The fitted fusion with the `zero` prior is kept as the default because it shrinks every family toward ignoring it and so cannot do materially worse than p0 by construction, not because it measured a gain. Whether G2 is reported as not met or the system is changed in phase B is the team's decision: {{TBD:g2_decision}}. The calibration of the fused posterior against p0 is {{TBD:fusion_calibration}}.

**How it is tested.** Synthetic worlds in `tests/test_fusion.py` and `tests/test_fusion_diagnostics.py` check that independent answers give weights near 1 and the same gain as naive fusion, that answers which copy p0 are detected, and that on double-counted data naive loses Brier while the CV-chosen fusion stays within 0.0001 of p0. The **shuffled-answers test** permutes the answers across alerts and cross-fits the fusion; the gain must fall to about zero ({{TBD:shuffled_answers}}), which shows that no gain comes from leakage.

## 5.4.5 Decision and stopping

The decision (`ftm/decision/threshold.py`) is one threshold on the fused probability: escalate if p ≥ t, otherwise clear. Because the posterior is calibrated, the cost-optimal threshold for costs c_fn and c_fp is c_fp / (c_fp + c_fn), and `fit_threshold` checks it on validation. It supports three objectives: minimum expected cost, F1 of the escalate class, and a daily review capacity (the lowest threshold whose escalations stay within a given number per day). It is fitted on validation alerts that went through the same earlier steps, conformal intake and fusion, as the alerts it will decide, and it is fixed in `prereg/` before the test set is touched.

The stop rule is checked after every answer, in this order: **budget** (no ε left for the smallest question, no LLM calls, or no simulated time); **fast-model clear** (the fast model or the 8B says the case is clear); **cannot cross** (no remaining question can move the posterior across the threshold). The last uses the privacy ceiling: an answer bought with ε shifts the log-odds by at most ε·Δ, with Δ = 1 for a yes/no answer and the typical launderer-versus-clean gap for a count, and fusion scales this by at most the largest weight, so all remaining questions together move the log-odds by at most `max_weight · Δ · ε_left`. If the posterior is farther than that from the threshold, asking more cannot change the decision and the agent stops. Budget comes first, so a stop is never blamed on the evidence. Because fitted weights can exceed 1, the bound is only valid if `max_weight` is set to the largest fitted weight.

The three-way decision of FCAC [40] (auto-clear, human review, auto-escalate, with a random 5% of past cases revealed after a delay to simulate audits) is a stretch item and is not part of the core; if it is built, its error rates are reported as controlled only under its own assumption ({{TBD:three_way}}).

**How it is tested.** `tests/test_decision.py` checks that the vectorised threshold fit matches brute force, that the cost fit recovers the Bayes threshold on calibrated scores, that the capacity threshold holds on every day, and the order of the stop reasons (budget first, then the fast model, then the ceiling). Section 7.4 reports the achieved error rates of the threshold on the test alerts.

## 5.4.6 External agents ported by T4

Section 6.2 describes why and how every external system is run; this part describes the ports T4 wrote. The licence and code status of each paper was checked in week 0 (`docs/external_code_check.md`) and decides how a system is built.

**Sharma [3]** (core) is a faithful port of the authors' MIT-licensed code (`ftm/external/sharma.py`, MIT notice kept). It keeps the bounded tool loop (at most 8 iterations, also capped by the shared call limit), the system prompt's rules, the four evidence tools (mapped onto what our alerts carry: the aliased alert summary, the one-bank score with its top feature attributions, the shape of the home bank's evidence, and the two nearest launder and two nearest clean train alerts) and the escalation policy that overrides the agent when the one-bank score is confident (at or above 0.65 or at or below 0.35) and the agent disagrees. Two variants run: `agent` (the agent alone, which is the failure case of [3]) and `governed` (with the escalation policy). Two adaptations are fixed before any run: native function calling becomes one schema-constrained JSON tool call per LLM call, because our serving stack uses guided decoding, and its "fraud / not fraud / escalate" verdict is mapped to a probability for Brier scoring with the per-class laundering rate on the validation fit part (defaults 0.9, 0.1 and p0).

**Torres et al. [6]** (core) is a **reimplementation**: the paper's code, prompts and backbone are not released, so the prompts are written from its section 3 and published with the port (`ftm/external/torres.py`). It builds an evidence bundle with per-type quotas (policy 3, context 1, transactions 20, similar cases 4), returns a structured decision (dismiss, monitor, escalate) with cited evidence ids, runs a verifier that rejects non-existent citations and wrong amounts or dates and triggers one repair call, and measures evidence counterfactuals without letting them change the decision, because the paper does not say how they would. The stated confidence goes to the chosen disposition, the rest is split equally over the other two, and the probability of laundering is P(escalate) + 0.5 · P(monitor). The paper's own numbers use another dataset (SynthAML10) and cannot be checked on AMLworld, so the port is labelled a reimplementation in Table 5a and only our numbers are reported.

Both run in two modes. **Native** is as published: the one-bank view, no partner banks. **Ported** gives the agent `ask_bank` through the same shared investigation layer, gate, accountant and caps as our planner, with one question round of up to three questions whose answers become evidence items.

**Lighter Group D ports** (secondary): multi-agent debate [55] (a reimplementation from the paper, because the official repository has no licence; three agents, two rounds after the first, mean of the last probabilities), Uncertainty of Thoughts [57] and BED-LLM [10] (both reimplementations, scoring each candidate question by expected information gain on a binary launder or clean belief, over three candidates per step). AG2/AutoGen [56] is not used for the debate because its own LLM calls would bypass the shared call cap. Every setting that the papers leave open is recorded in `prereg/external_list.yaml` under `fixed_choices` before the system's first run, and none is tuned after seeing results. Group C's secondary systems (Co-Investigator AI [45], FinFRE-RAG [28]) are T2's and the remaining Group D systems are T3's (sections 5.2 and 5.3).

**How it is tested.** The ports have unit tests on the mock LLM (`tests/test_sharma_port.py`, `tests/test_torres_port.py`, `tests/test_debate_port.py`, `tests/test_group_d_secondary.py`, `tests/test_groupc_ports.py`); they run the loops and the call caps without a real model. A real-endpoint run on a small validation sample is part of the pre-run checks; the end-to-end and questions-only scores of every port are {{TBD:tab_5a}}.
